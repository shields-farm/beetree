# Description

Wax built in unwanted places — between frames, on top bars, on the inner cover, around feeders.

# Action

Scrape burr comb during inspection to keep frames separable and prevent cross comb. Assess whether the colony needs more space (add a box, pull honey frames). If the colony is a chronic comb-builder across seasons, note the genetics — you may not want to raise queens from this line.

# Causes

- out of space (confidence 0.5) — colony has nowhere to store/lay and is filling gaps opportunistically
- proactive genetics (confidence 0.35) — some lines are just comb-happy — they build wax constantly regardless of space
- both (confidence 0.15) — proactive genetics + crowding = maximum burr comb

# Notes

Burr comb is not a disease or a problem by itself — it's the bees doing what bees do: filling available space with comb. The question is WHY they have available space. If frames are properly spaced and boxes are adequate, burr comb means either (1) they need more room — add a super or another brood box, or (2) they're genetically inclined to build excess wax — some feral-derived lines do this persistently. Check: Is the brood nest backfilled with nectar/honey (congested → give space)? Is there an empty box on top they could be drawing instead (yes → they chose to build in the gap, it's genetics)? If you're managing for honey production, chronic burr comb lines are annoying but manageable with frequent scraping. If you're breeding, you might not want to propagate that trait.
